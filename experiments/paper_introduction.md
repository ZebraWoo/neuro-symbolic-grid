# I. Introduction

The accelerating integration of renewable energy sources—particularly wind and solar photovoltaic (PV) generation—into modern power grids has fundamentally altered the operational landscape of electricity systems. Unlike conventional dispatchable generation, renewable generation exhibits strong stochasticity, multi-timescale volatility, and weather-dependent uncertainty [1-3]. In regions with high renewable penetration, grid operators face a rapidly evolving decision environment where load-generation imbalances can materialize within minutes, and extreme meteorological events such as typhoons, heatwaves, or sudden cloud transients can trigger cascading frequency excursions and voltage violations [4,5].

In this context, the central challenge is no longer merely accurate forecasting of renewable output or load demand. Rather, it is **how to translate heterogeneous, high-dimensional grid observations into timely, physically compliant, and interpretable decision recommendations** that assist human operators in maintaining system security. This paper addresses the problem of **intelligent decision support for power grid operations** under high renewable uncertainty.

Existing approaches to data-driven grid intelligence can be broadly classified into three paradigms, each with fundamental limitations. **Physics-based optimization methods**, such as optimal power flow (OPF) and model predictive control (MPC), rely on explicit system models and guarantee constraint satisfaction, but their computational burden scales poorly with system complexity and stochasticity, rendering them unsuitable for real-time decision support under rapidly changing conditions [6,7]. **Deep learning (DL) methods**, including recurrent neural networks (RNNs), temporal convolutional networks (TCNs), and Transformer architectures, have demonstrated strong capability in capturing nonlinear temporal dependencies from operational data [8-10]. However, these models operate as black-box function approximators: their outputs may violate fundamental physical constraints—such as Kirchhoff's current law (KCL), ramp rate limits, and state-of-charge (SOC) boundaries of energy storage systems—and they offer limited interpretability regarding *why* a particular recommendation was made [11,12]. **Hybrid physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)** partially address the physical compliance gap by embedding differential equation constraints into the loss function [13,14]. Nevertheless, PINNs typically encode only continuous physics equations and cannot incorporate the discrete, qualitative expert knowledge—operational heuristics, safety protocols, and contingency rules—that constitutes a substantial portion of practical grid operational intelligence [15].

A promising yet underexplored direction lies at the intersection of two emerging AI paradigms: **spiking neural networks (SNNs)** and **neuro-symbolic learning**. SNNs, also termed brain-inspired or neuromorphic models, process information through discrete spike events governed by biologically plausible neuronal dynamics such as the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) or Izhikevich models [16,17]. Their event-driven computation offers inherent advantages in energy efficiency and temporal processing compared to traditional artificial neural networks (ANNs) [18]. However, the application of SNNs to power systems remains nascent, with existing work limited primarily to fault detection and load forecasting tasks [19,20]. Neuro-symbolic learning, on the other hand, aims to combine the pattern recognition capabilities of neural networks with the logical rigor and interpretability of symbolic reasoning [21,22]. By encoding domain knowledge as differentiable logical constraints, neuro-symbolic systems can produce outputs that are both data-driven and rule-compliant—a property of paramount importance for safety-critical infrastructure such as power grids.

In this paper, we propose a **neuro-symbolic closed-loop learning framework for intelligent power grid decision support** that bridges these two paradigms for the first time in the power systems domain. The framework ingests multimodal operational data—including SCADA measurements, renewable generation readings, and meteorological variables—and outputs a set of **five interpretable decision intents** (Increase Generation, Charge ESS, Discharge ESS, Voltage Support, and Risk Warning) that serve as actionable recommendations to human operators. The key architectural innovations are:

1. **Multi-compartment spiking neural encoder**: We design a biologically inspired SNN architecture in which distinct dendritic compartments receive modality-specific spike-encoded inputs (rate coding for continuous electrical quantities, temporal coding for meteorological transients), while a somatic Izhikevich neuron integrates these signals through coupled nonlinear dynamics. This multi-compartment structure provides richer temporal feature extraction than conventional single-compartment LIF neurons, without the computational cost of full Hodgkin-Huxley models.

2. **Differentiable symbolic rule layer**: We formalize a set of operational heuristics (e.g., "if renewable generation surges, charge energy storage"; "if state-of-charge exceeds the upper bound, prohibit charging") as expert targets, and employ a temperature-scaled sigmoid logic neuron to map the gap between the model's decision intent and the expert target to a continuous soft truth value in [0,1]. A differentiable product t-norm operator aggregates all rule truth values into a comprehensive trust score, which is incorporated as a regularization term in the training objective.

3. **Physics-constrained loss formulation**: Beyond symbolic rules, we encode four fundamental physical constraints—ramp rate limits, SOC bounds, power balance, and curtailment caps—as differentiable penalty terms in the loss function. This ensures that the model's decision recommendations remain within physically feasible operating envelopes without requiring explicit grid simulation.

4. **Closed-loop consistency feedback mechanism**: Inspired by the fast-slow dual-loop correction architecture from control theory, we introduce a closed-loop inference procedure. After an initial forward pass generates decision intents, a constraint verification step computes residual violation signals, which are fed back to correct the output features via gradient-based fast-loop adjustment. The corrected features then undergo re-verification, iterating until convergence. This mechanism significantly reduces physics and rule violations at inference time without requiring real-time grid feedback.

We evaluate the proposed framework on the PSML (Power Systems Machine Learning) dataset, which comprises minute-resolution load, renewable generation, and meteorological data across 66 zones from major U.S. interconnection regions. Comprehensive experiments across seven evaluation dimensions demonstrate that: (i) the proposed framework achieves superior decision accuracy compared to five baseline architectures (LSTM, Transformer, TCN, single-compartment LIF-SNN, and single-compartment Izhikevich-SNN); (ii) the neuro-symbolic rule layer improves rule satisfaction rates to above 90% while reducing physics violations by over 80% compared to unconstrained models; (iii) the closed-loop mechanism converges within five iterations, monotonically reducing residual violations; and (iv) ablation studies confirm the indispensable contribution of each architectural component. A detailed interpretability case study further demonstrates that the model's decision logic is transparently traceable to specific activated rules, providing human operators with auditable reasoning chains.
